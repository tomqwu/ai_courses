Question 4 of 8Objective: M7.2

Your macOS app sells at ~$69 one-time through your own checkout. A store listing would add reach, take a cut of every sale, push subscription expectations, and own the customer record. Choosing "by channel economics" means:

Why

MacWhisper is the natural experiment: the same app is ~€59/$69 one-time on Gumroad and $6.99/mo–$99.99 lifetime in the App Store (ListenToMe/docs/competition-analysis.md:34), so the channel changes the model, not just the sticker. Question 6 of 8Objective: M3.1

Your desktop copilot runs on-device by default. Users who paste their own API key cost you nothing per month, and so do users served by the platform's private-cloud model while your app qualifies for its free tier; a managed cloud option costs you about $8.00 per active user per month at your assumed token rate. Which pricing structure matches these costs?

Question 6 of 8: choose one answer

Why

Price the way each cost recurs, tier by tier: on-device, bring-your-own-key and eligible private-cloud users cost nothing monthly, so a one-time price fits them (the BYOK discount) — though only the first may be sold as "on-device" (M3.1's three tiers); only the managed cloud tier recurs, so only it is monthly, priced above its line and capped (hybrid) — the M7.2 TinyCopilot floor (course/03-content/m07-monetize/lesson.md). A taxes on-device users for a cost they do not cause — the audit a buyer can run; C takes the price from the band without the cost structure; D meters compute you do not pay for. (M7.1 — pricing taxonomy: what each model matches and punishes)

Question 7 of 8Objective: M7.3

Your product: a local-first CLI that drafts release notes from git history, runs fully offline, MIT-licensed, with a paid hosted tier for teams. Write the positioning one-liner in the ListenToMe format (adjective-wedge × differentiators × audience), add one "what this doesn't do" claim you would publish, and explain in one sentence why that claim increases conversion.

Model answer

"the free, open-source, fully offline release-notes copilot for small teams — bring your own repo, keep your history private, draft notes in one command." Each clause must map to a table column or capability (free/open-source ← price/license row; fully offline ← privacy column; bring your own repo ← BYO; one command ← workflow). Honest claim, e.g.: "It drafts factual, commit-derived notes — it does not invent roadmap promises your commits don't support." Why it converts: qualified claims are a premium signal — a buyer who reads the limitation stops asking "why believe you" and starts asking "when do we start" (course/00-research/03-ai-qe-deep-read.md §3). — M7.3 — one-liner derivation; honest-claim conversion logic

Question 8 of 8Objective: M7.1

A meeting-transcription web app sends audio to a cloud ASR vendor at $0.30 per hour of audio and stores transcripts on your servers. Assume a typical user transcribes 20 hours a month and payments cost 5% + $0.50 per charge. Compute the per-user inference-cost line and the lowest monthly price that covers it, then choose a pricing model and the first tier's price, and justify in three sentences using one comparator rule from this module.

Model answer

inference-cost line 20 h × $0.30 = $6.00 per user per month, recurring; covering it after fees needs 0.95 × price − $0.50 ≥ $6.00, so price ≥ $6.85. Model: per-user subscription with a 20-hour allowance (hybrid), ~$12–15/user/mo for the first tier, leaving ~$4.90–7.75 per user toward the fixed floor. (1) Per-user cloud ASR cost recurs monthly, so a one-time price would be a subsidy that grows with use — subscription matches the cost structure, and the allowance caps the heavy user (40 hours costs $12.00). (2) The first tier sits inside Otter's ~$8.33–16.99/user/mo Pro band and at Granola's ~$14 Business price — within the comparator band your table shows, above the floor. (3) The comparator rule: the price buys defined limits (hours transcribed, seats, retention) and is sourced, per the pricing-table convention (ListenToMe/docs/competition-analysis.md). — M7.1/M7.2 — compute the inference-cost line, then choose model + first tier with a comparator rule
