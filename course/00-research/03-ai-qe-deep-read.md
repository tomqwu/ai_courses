# Deep-Read Report: ai_qe (The Expertise-Content Product)

> Source: subagent deep-read of the `ai_qe/` clone, a sibling of `course/` in the workspace root (all claims grounded in files read directly from the repo).
> Archetype framing ("3rd type of app") is the course's framing, not the repo's — but the repo is a near-perfect specimen of that archetype.

## 1. What this product is, and the archetype lesson

AI × QE (`README.md`; published at tomqwu.github.io/ai_qe) is a Jekyll + just-the-docs static site, deployed by GitHub Actions to GitHub Pages, about "modernizing quality engineering and adding AI assistance around reliable testing" in regulated financial services. On the surface it is a content site: four narrated decks, a fintech case study, a dictionary, research pages, downloadable PDFs. What makes it the "third type of app" — neither a tool nor generic content — is that it is **packaged expertise with an explicit sales funnel**. The product is not software; it is trust, engineered like software.

The funnel is visible in `index.md`: four path cards route the visitor — 01/VISION (executive briefings), 02/THE STORY (the fintech case: "75 offshore QA staff. A payment retry."), 03/ARCHITECTURE (technical decks), 04/NEXT STEP (`/discovery/` — "Plan the discovery"). The terminal page is a consulting engagement: `_data/engagement.json` records a staged timeline (2 weeks sponsor alignment; 4–6 weeks baseline; 8–10 weeks capped pilot; 1–2 quarters validation) and the proposed commercial basis: "fixed-fee or capped discovery; separately capped pilot… No client price or start date has been agreed."

The archetype lesson: one practitioner's research base + methodology + presentation craft became a versioned, tested, evidence-cited product that simultaneously (a) demonstrates competence, (b) pre-answers objections, (c) qualifies leads (the questionnaire), and (d) pre-sells the engagement. The critical enabler is stated in `README.md`: "Our Banking Client is an illustrative payment-testing scenario. Planning inputs and proposed outcomes are not observed client results. Modernization gains, AI contribution, capacity and cash are measured separately." Honesty is the differentiator that makes the product sellable.

## 2. Audience-routing / content architecture

**One body of research, four decks.** `_data/briefing_room.json` defines four presentations: Banking scenario Executive (21 slides, 25–30 min), Banking scenario Technical (33 slides, 35–45 min), Industry perspective Executive (26 slides), Industry perspective Technical (36 slides) — 116 slides total. Strategy vs. implementation × executive vs. technical: the same evidence base is re-cut per audience.

**Guided vs. full routes.** `_data/briefing_routes.json` defines curated sequences over the same stable slide IDs (e.g., the banking executive route plays a subset with a decision-discussion closer). "Focused routes end on a decision discussion; full decks retain the supporting material" (`README.md`). The route is shareable via `?route=client#slide-1`; the presentation room (`briefings/index.md`) filters via `?for=evp` / `?for=technical` and embeds a live deck preview. It even scripts the sales meeting: "A suggested 30-minute conversation" — Align 5 min / Explore 15 min / Agree 10 min — ending at discovery.

**PDF/narration/diagram synchronization.** Every deck has matching full and guided PDFs (10 current PDFs total: 4 full decks, 4 guided routes, research companion, questionnaire). All 116 slides have narration: 116 scripts totalling 57,182 characters, 114 unique MP3s (`maintainers/narration.md`). "Recorded narration, English captions and diagram flow share one clock" (`README.md`): `audio.currentTime` is the story clock; caption-anchored cues in `assets/data/narration-flows.json` drive diagram highlights (78 cues across 12 recordings at v1.19.0); the technical platform walkthrough is 4m50s / 72 measured captions / 13 synchronized sections covering 11 components. One audio-focus controller pauses other players across tabs and windows (BroadcastChannel).

**Release discipline.** `_data/release.yml` separates five edition fields: site `version: 1.24.1`, `slide_edition: 1.24.0`, `fintech_edition: 1.24.0`, `questionnaire_edition: 4`, `research_edition: 1.7.0`. A player-only patch advances the version while retaining slide-content and PDF editions. `releases.md` is a changelog where every entry states what changed **and what was deliberately retained**. Public content changes require a new edition before deployment; superseded editions live in immutable GitHub releases. Source publication dates stay separate from site release dates.

## 3. Evidence discipline

This is the site's core credibility engine. The rules (from `CONTRIBUTING.md` "Research conventions"): no client/partner names; "research sources are cited by name because the citation is the evidence"; every benchmark record includes date, sample, method, unit, self-reported vs. measured, sponsor, and what claim it supports; "new research goes in `docs/research-log.md` first (dated entry), then into the topic page."

- **`docs/research-log.md`** — dated entries with Question / Checked / Outcome / Changed / Open evidence. It records corrections made *during* verification, an explicit "Not verified" list (WQR cost-of-quality share, any Gartner productivity figure, Snyk accuracy…), and standing open questions. Research is logged before pages are edited.
- **`docs/evidence/benchmarks.md` / `testing-studies.md`** — every row "checked against the cited page on the verification date shown," with expandable sample/method details, sponsor, and a "Supports:" tag limiting the claim type. `docs/evidence/index.md`: each source is tagged with the claim type it can legitimately support (task efficiency / capacity / cost avoidance / hard-dollar saving); "self-reported figures are never used as measured ones"; paywalled figures are noted as coming from press releases, and inaccessible analyst notes are simply not cited.
- **Provenance files** — `research/document-manifest.json` records 11 retrievals with SHA-256 hashes, and honestly logs one McKinsey PDF as "unavailable / read operation timed out." `research/visual-provenance.md` preserves the exact ImageGen prompts for the two hero illustrations. `maintainers/narration.md` + `assets/data/narration-provenance.json` record voice/model provenance.
- **Remediation reviews (`research/reviews/`)** — `site-audit-2026-09-06.md` is a published self-audit: 14 findings (4 high, 9 medium, 1 lower) with severity, evidence paths, and acceptance criteria. `remediation-2026-09-06.md` maps every finding → implemented response → verification. `review-56-resolution-2026-09-10.md` records accepted PR #56 scope, deliberate non-changes, and deferred suggestions — including "Do not hide weak economics, convert capacity to cash, or relabel unknown results as observed."
- **Narration review gate** — `tools/narration-review.cjs` hashes each rendered slide with its script, recording and caption-flow definitions; semantic changes require an explicit `retained` or `refreshed` decision with a reason. "Never reset the baseline to silence a stale-review failure" (`CONTRIBUTING.md`).
- **The signature qualifier** — "planning inputs are not observed client results" (`README.md`). Baseline and observed-after results are repeatedly marked "not recorded"; illustrative scenarios are labelled illustrative everywhere, including in the narration scripts.

Why this matters: the site is deliberately *more skeptical than its audience*. An executive who reads "the only independent RCT is negative" on a vendor deck's own site stops asking "why should I believe you?" and starts asking "when can we start?"

## 4. The consulting methodology docs (`docs/method/`)

Written to be "reused engagement after engagement… Names and numbers specific to any one engagement have been removed" (`docs/method/index.md`).

- **`discovery-questionnaire.md`** — the lead-qualification asset. Design rules: one form, role-routed at the first question; ranges mutually exclusive and gap-free; single-select on the three questions that define success (primary outcome, autonomy ceiling, go/no-go threshold); the activity taxonomy matches the savings formula so answers feed the model directly. It includes a forensic post-mortem of a failed 29-question form: 186 checkbox options, real completion 20–30 minutes, no single respondent can answer all six sections, and the "largest omission was the financial-capture question set." The v4 form is 36 questions; sponsor route ~11–13 min, engineering route ~15–25 min.
- **`interview-and-data-request.md`** — a 45–60 minute interview guide across six topics plus a "minimum data request" table (9 items, ranges acceptable). It converts questionnaire ranges into a baseline without demanding precision.
- **`phased-pilot.md`** — five phases (0–4), each with objective, duration, person-day ranges ("deliberately not converted to dollars"), deliverables, and a go/no-go gate signed by the sponsor. Two default use cases (failed-test triage; executable API/component test generation) with reasons. Measurement design: frozen acceptance criteria before observing results; alternate sprints/squads; net effort including review, correction and control effort; complexity and developer experience as covariates ("the evidence shows they change the sign of the effect"). Exact decision boundaries: 10%/15% effort bands, ≥30 comparable tasks per arm, confidence intervals crossing a boundary are "insufficient evidence," one extension of ≤4 weeks. A benefits-realization register ties every claimed saving to a Finance-owned budget row. Lesson: sell measurement, not outcomes.
- **`executive-presentation.md`** — a 60–90 minute workshop script: 10 timed segments, starting with the sponsor's own concerns, showing questionnaire results as "questions rather than conclusions; no savings number yet," and ending with specific decisions requested (named sponsor, Finance partner, one or two applications, Phase 0–1 ceiling). The decision requested "is whether to fund Phases 0 and 1, not whether to transform QA" (`docs/economics/slide-language.md`).

Together these teach: enterprises buy a bounded, evidence-first path — and the product pre-frames the conversation so the consultant never has to promise a number.

## 5. Engineered like software

- **`make check`** (`Makefile`) = models → build → site → browser: npm model tests, Python unittest discovery, narration validation with `--require-complete`, contract validation, Jekyll build + finalize, and browser QA.
- **Browser QA suites** — `tools/qa-groups.json` owns five CI-identical groups (playback, flows, site, models, architecture), run in both Chromium and WebKit, at 1280×720, 1920×1080 and 375×812. Tests exercise user-visible failure modes: real clicks, keyboard selection, modifier-opened tabs, focus, real audio completion, cross-tab audio focus — a direct response to audit finding R14 ("release checks do not cover the failures the audience encounter").
- **Validators** — `verify_site.py`, `verify_industry.py`, `verify_pdf.py` (PDF field integrity), `verify_qe_scope.py` (keeps retired material out of publication), plus pure model unit tests. "The link check does not treat a successful HTTP response as evidence that a claim is correct."
- **Immutable releases** — `tools/prepare_release.py` assembles the ten PDFs, MP4, captions, CSV/JSON source registers and checksums; `publish_release.py` verifies GitHub's SHA-256 digests and "publishes only after all assets are complete"; "an existing published edition is never overwritten." `publication_scope.py` separates validation from deployment intent.
- **Data as single source of truth** — canonical `_data/*.json` (scenarios, pilot gates, briefing routes, sources, dictionary) with derived tables regenerated by script; the site's economics, gates and slide counts all reconcile by construction.
- **Edition discipline** — content changes without a new edition "are rejected on main before deployment"; superseded editions remain in immutable GitHub releases.

The lesson "treat content like code" is fully realized here: content diffs carry versions, tests, release notes, review gates, and immutable artifacts.

## 6. Concrete teachable takeaways (with file pointers)

1. **Route one deck to multiple audiences.** Curated guided sequences over stable slide IDs, ending on a decision (`_data/briefing_routes.json`, `briefings/index.md`).
2. **Build a provenance trail for every claim.** Retrieval status + SHA-256 for each source (`research/document-manifest.json`), image prompts (`research/visual-provenance.md`), voice provenance (`maintainers/narration.md`).
3. **Log research before you publish it.** Dated Question/Checked/Outcome/Changed entries, plus "not verified" and "open questions" sections (`docs/research-log.md`).
4. **Label the epistemic status of every number.** Four levels of "saving" (`docs/principles.md`); "planning inputs are not observed client results" (`README.md`); exact wording that can and cannot appear on a slide (`docs/economics/slide-language.md`).
5. **Separate editions like software releases** — and record what each release deliberately retains (`_data/release.yml`, `releases.md`).
6. **Publish your own audit and remediation.** Finding → response → verification tables build more trust than any testimonial (`research/reviews/site-audit-2026-09-06.md`, `remediation-2026-09-06.md`).
7. **Turn a questionnaire into a sales asset.** Role-routed, taxonomy-aligned, with the financial-capture questions that make a pilot fundable (`docs/method/discovery-questionnaire.md`).
8. **Sell the decision, not the transformation.** "The decision today is whether to fund the first two phases, not whether to transform QA" (`docs/economics/slide-language.md`, `docs/method/executive-presentation.md`).
9. **Bind media to meaning with review gates.** Hash rendered slides against scripts/recordings/captions; require deliberate retained/refreshed decisions; never reset a baseline to silence a failure (`tools/narration-review.cjs`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`).
10. **Make content changes require tests to pass.** `make check`, five browser groups, PDF and scope validators, release preflight (`Makefile`, `tools/README.md`).

## 7. Facts/numbers worth citing

- **Scale of the product:** 116 slides across 4 decks; 116 narration scripts / 57,182 spoken characters / 114 unique MP3s; 10 current PDFs; 62 dictionary terms across 6 categories; 30 curated source records (`_data/industry_sources.json`); 7 fintech cases from 10 sources; site version 1.24.1 (2026-09-10).
- **Evidence numbers:** METR early-2025 RCT: 19% *longer* completion (CI +2% to +39%); developers expected 24% faster and believed 20% faster after; 16 maintainers, 246 issues. METR Feb-2026 follow-up: −18% and −4% point estimates, both CIs cross zero; 57 devs, 800+ tasks. Peng 2023: 55.8% faster (95 freelancers, one synthetic task, vendor-affiliated). Cui et al.: +26.1% completed tasks (SE 10.3). GitHub/Accenture: +8.7% PRs. Bain: 10–15% average gains, rarely monetized. WQR: 19% self-reported. Atlassian: coding = 16% of developer time; Bain: code generation = 25–35% of idea-to-launch — so a 50% task gain "dilutes to single digits of total engineering time."
- **Testing-specific studies:** Meta TestGen-LLM 75%/57%/25% class-level cumulative yields; Uber FlakyGuard: 47.6% of *reproducible* flaky tests fixed, 51.8% of fixes accepted, only 71.6% reproducible; Google ICSE 2026 triage: 90.1% accuracy on 71 manually evaluated failures, deployed on 52,635 failing tests; CATGen: 91.8% compilation success vs 51–76% baselines.
- **The economics model** (`docs/economics/savings-model.md`): illustrative base = 3.3% capacity released (55%×60%×50%×20%), 0.45% net cash ($45,000 per $10M); zero-capture scenario = −1.2% (−$120,000 per $10M); review-slowdown scenario = −$450,000 economic impact. Release-pack arithmetic: 300→222→78 hours (26%)→66 after operations→33 usable, recovering a 480-hour setup in 15 packs.
- **Pilot gates** (`_data/pilot_gates.json`): 15% net-effort go threshold, 10% review band, 50% adoption floor, 3-week baseline, 8-week pilot, 2 observed releases, ≥30 tasks per arm, ≤4-week extension; six sponsor-selectable objectives (effort, cash, quality, reliability, evidence, reuse).
- **Case & method:** 75 offshore QA staff (45 manual/domain testers); nine explicit AI-assisted handoffs; twelve adoption dependencies; six QE modernization workstreams; questionnaire v4 = 36 questions (the failed original: 29 questions, 186 options, 20–30 minutes).
- **Governance anchors:** OSFI B-10/B-13/E-21, E-23 effective 1 May 2027, July 2026 Technology Risk Bulletin on generative/agentic AI, FIFAI II (March 2026), FSB consultation (June 2026), 24-hour incident reporting, Bill C-36 fines up to the higher of $25M or 5% of global revenue.

**Bottom line for the course:** AI × QE demonstrates that a solo expert can build a sellable "third type of app" — a research-backed, audience-routed, evidence-disciplined presentation site with consulting attached — provided they treat credibility as the product, content as code, and every claim as an artifact with provenance.
