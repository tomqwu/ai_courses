# Facilitation — M8 Live Session: The Launch Clinic (90 minutes)

> One 90-minute workshop, cohort week 8, run before demo day. Participants arrive with a draft sales
> page and a first pass at the seven emails. The clinic fixes sections, phases, and the deadline — it
> does not re-teach the module. Formula is **I do / We do / You do** per `02-instructor/instructor-guide.md`
> §2–3, split roughly 30/25/30 with 5 minutes of close.

## Timing table

| Minutes | Activity | Mode | Artifacts |

| 0–2 | Opening hook: the 1% → 8% restructure | I do | Research §E on screen |

## Opening hook (2 minutes)

"One creator had a page that converted at one percent. They restructured it onto eight sections — same offer, same price, same audience — and it converted at eight percent. Nothing was invented; the proof came from work that already shipped. That is the bet of this session: your page does not need better adjectives, it needs the right eight moves in the right order, each fed by an artifact you already own. By minute 85 you will have audited one page against those rows and phased your own arc. Open `04-sales/landing-page.md` now."

## I do (2–26): the two moves that carry the page

Walk the eight sections on the course's page, spending the most time on section 4 and section 8. Read aloud one bad outcome line ("Week 3 covers privacy") and one good one ("Week 3: you'll ship a hardened local-only mode"), then do the same for the CTA: show the two different enroll buttons a student typically writes, and collapse them to one repeated line. Then rewrite a volunteer's headline live — accept the first version, name what is unfalsifiable about it, and rewrite once. Do not polish. Model the recovery.

## Breakout instructions (26–46)

- **Groups:** pairs, assigned by the platform's random pairing; 20 minutes, hard stop.
- **Roles:** *Author* reads their eight sections aloud in order and stops talking. *Auditor* scores each row and speaks only in the audit format.
- **Deliverable each pair posts:** the eight-row audit table plus the single highest-leverage fix.
- **Exact prompt:** "Open your partner's draft. For each of the eight sections, write one line: present / weak / missing, and the artifact that should feed it. Then find the claim on their page you cannot source, and name the M1–M7 artifact that would make it honest — or write the placeholder sentence. Do not rewrite their copy. Post the table and the one fix you would make first. Time: 20 minutes; I will call the stop."

## Discussion prompts (46–56 and threaded after)

1. **"Which section is doing someone else's job on your page?"** Probe: *what would you move out of it?* A strong answer names the mechanism — a bio selling the transformation, or a pricing section re-arguing the problem — not a vague "it's long."
2. **"Which of your three warmup emails sells?"** Probe: *where is the link, and what does it cost you later?* A strong answer removes the link or reclassifies the email as conversion, and says how the free tool carries the ask instead.
3. **"If nobody buys, is your deadline still real?"** Probe: *what actually changes on day 15?* A strong answer states a concrete change — cart closes, founding price ends — and accepts that extending it once is a decision with a cost, not a kindness.
4. **"What is your capstone's single weakest dimension right now?"** Probe: *which named artifact would raise it?* A strong answer names a dimension and an artifact ("evidence honesty — I have no date on any run"), not "I need to do more."

## Watch-fors (30-second interventions)

| Stuck point | Symptom | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Scope explosion | Student describes three products or "all three archetypes" | Say the instructor-guide line: "ONE archetype, ONE shippable scope, the loop complete. The rubric scores the loop, not the size." |
| Testimonial temptation | Draft quotes appear on the page | "There are no testimonials yet. Copy the reserved-slot pattern and the badge strip. A founder-written quote is fabrication." |
| Topic-list curriculum | Section 4 reads like a syllabus | Point at the outcome table in `04-sales/landing-page.md`; rewrite one line live. |
| Fake urgency | "Last chance" recurring, or a resetting countdown | Point at the no-fake-countdowns line in `04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`; replace with the founding-price end date. |
| Page too long or too short | 3,000 words at a $149 price, or 700 words at $1,490 | Re-derive the target from the research §E bands and cut or add to the objection section. |
| Evidence-record vagueness | "All tests pass" in the record | Ask for the command and its counts; if there is no red run, that is the finding to record. |

## Close (85–90)

"Three things to do before you build. Post your plan — one archetype, one sentence of scope, and each loop stage mapped to a named artifact — and review one peer's plan by naming the artifact they left vague. Set your page's word target from your price now, not after you draft. And decide the one lever you will invest in first: list, opens, clicks, the page, or price. Demo day is next; the hour that scores you is also the hour that fills your page's reserved testimonial slots. Post your audit table to the module thread before you leave."

## Post-session checklist

- [ ] Record attendance and each pair's posted audit table as session evidence.
- [ ] Log the three most common weak sections across the class; re-teach if one exceeds 30%.
- [ ] Confirm every student has a named archetype and scope before demo day.
- [ ] Flag any draft page carrying an unsourced number or an invented testimonial, and follow up privately.
- [ ] Post to the community: the winning before/after headline pair, the timing table, and the demo-day schedule.
- [ ] Note stuck points and interventions to the instructor-guide table before the next cohort.
