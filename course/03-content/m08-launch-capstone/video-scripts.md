# Video Scripts — M8: Launch: Sales Page, Email Arc, Capstone

> Three recording scripts, one per segment. Narration is written to be read aloud at ~130 words/min.
> Each segment's beat table allots spoken narration plus on-screen demo time; word budgets below count
> **spoken** narration only, so a 5-minute segment is roughly 520 spoken words plus screen work.
> Open the pointed files on screen rather than describing them.

## M8.1 — The sales page

**Target runtime:** 5:00 · **Spoken budget:** ≈520 words at 130 wpm · **Segment:** `03-content/m08-launch-capstone/lesson.md` M8.1

**Cold open (≈15 s).** One creator moved a sales page from 1% to 8% conversion by restructuring it onto eight sections. The sections were not new. The order was. Here is the order, and here is the course's own page as the worked example.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | `04-sales/landing-page.md` hero | "This is a real page for a real offer, and every claim on it has a file behind it. We are going to read it as a sequence of eight moves, because that is what it is." |
| 0:20 | The eight-section table | "Destination, qualification, stakes, outcomes, credibility, borrowed trust, objections, price. Each section has exactly one job. When a page underperforms, the fix is usually a section doing someone else's job." |
| 1:00 | Hero + for/isn't lists | "The headline sells a destination and is falsifiable: ship three real AI products. Then two explicit lists — who this is for, and who it is not for. The second list is not rudeness; it cuts refunds and protects completion." |
| 1:50 | Problem section, then curriculum table | "Now the stakes: three things are always missing. Then the curriculum, as outcomes. Watch the difference — this table lists abilities, not topics. Engineer a fail-closed local-only mode is a claim you can hold the author to." |
| 2:40 | Bio, then testimonials note | "The bio is 100 to 150 words with three named repos. Then the honest part: three reserved testimonial slots and a note saying the section stays honest. No invented social proof. For a technical audience, shipped projects are the proof." |
| 3:20 | FAQ + pricing tiers + CTA | "The FAQ answers real objections plainly, and pricing shows every tier — $399, $1,490, team from $2,500. One call to action, repeated. Not two. Competing buttons leak the click." |
| 4:10 | Length table | "Length follows price: 800 to 1,200 words under $200, 2,000 to 3,000 for a $500-plus or cold-traffic offer. This page is about 1,900 words of copy. Set your target from your price before you draft." |
| 4:40 | Blank document | "Open your draft beside this page and write section one now. One falsifiable sentence. Then the two lists." |

**Demo cue.** Scroll `04-sales/landing-page.md` in one continuous pass, pausing only on the reserved-testimonial note and the pricing tiers. The viewer should notice that no number appears without a file behind it.

**Action-step close.** Draft all eight sections for the product you priced in Lab M7, and tag every claim with its proof artifact from the M1–M7 inventory. This draft is capstone artifact 6.

**Recording notes.**
- Enlarge the curriculum table: the abilities-vs-topics contrast is the segment's most copyable move.
- If over time, cut the FAQ walkthrough; the pricing and testimonials beats carry more weight.
- Do not say "longer pages convert better" in any form — the research says the opposite in both directions.
- Never read a testimonial aloud; there are none yet. Read the reserved-slot note instead.

## M8.2 — The launch arc

**Target runtime:** 5:00 · **Spoken budget:** ≈520 words · **Segment:** `03-content/m08-launch-capstone/lesson.md` M8.2

**Cold open (≈15 s).** The email that sells is not the email that earns the right to sell. Seven emails, two phases, and a deadline you actually keep — that is the whole arc.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | `04-sales/launch-plan.md` timeline | "Two phases over about four weeks. Warmup at weeks minus four to minus one, while the page sits in notify-me mode. Then the cart opens for ten to fourteen days. The phase structure never changes." |
| 0:30 | The seven-email table | "Warmup is the origin story, the transformation proof, and the free tool. Conversion is cart open, objection teardown, proof, final call. One idea per email, one CTA per email, no emoji — these are written for a technical skeptic." |
| 1:20 | Email 1 and email 3 bodies | "Warmup does not sell. Not softly, not with a link at the bottom. The free tool delivers the method in ten minutes — that is the ask, and it is the only honest way to ask for attention before the cart opens." |
| 2:10 | The deadline slide | "Here is why the final forty-eight hours matter: 42 to 55 percent of enrollments land there. Deferred decisions collapse at a real deadline. Which makes the deadline load-bearing — and makes a fake countdown expensive, because it teaches your list to wait." |
| 2:50 | The revenue formula | "Model it before you run it. List times open times click times page conversion times price. Twelve hundred subscribers, 38 percent, 15 percent, 16 percent, $797 — about $8,767. Then record actuals and re-derive. The launch plan opens an evidence log for exactly this." |
| 3:30 | Deliverability checklist | "Upstream of all five numbers is deliverability. SPF, DKIM, DMARC configured before the first send, an opt-in-only list, hard bounces scrubbed, and a test to a Gmail and a corporate address. A spam-folder email has an open rate of zero." |
| 4:10 | Founding tier $990 / $1,490 | "And where email six's testimonial comes from: the beta trade. Founding tier at $990 against $1,490, exchanged for a testimonial and a feedback session, agreed at checkout. A discount with no stated reason trains buyers to wait." |
| 4:40 | Community post box | "Write your seven subject lines and your model, then post them. Twenty people on your list is a real number." |

**Demo cue.** Show the launch plan's operations checklist as a live file and read the deadline line out loud. The viewer should notice that every promise here has an owner and a date.

**Action-step close.** Write all seven emails — subject line, three-bullet outline, single CTA, phase label — state what happens when your deadline passes, and compute your model with your real list size.

**Recording notes.**
- Keep the timeline on screen for the entire first ninety seconds; the −4 to +3 structure is the takeaway.
- If short on time, compress the beta-trade beat into one sentence and keep deliverability.
- Do not quote any testimonial or claim any launch result — this course has not run a cohort yet.

## M8.3 — Capstone: ship and demo

**Target runtime:** 5:00 · **Spoken budget:** ≈520 words · **Segment:** `03-content/m08-launch-capstone/lesson.md` M8.3

**Cold open (≈15 s).** The capstone is not build something big. It is one archetype, one shippable scope, and the complete loop with evidence — because no rubric dimension counts lines of code.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Contract slide | "ONE archetype, ONE shippable scope, the loop complete. That is the instructor guide's unblock for the most common failure in this course: scope explosion. A small complete loop out-scores a sprawling half-loop on every dimension." |
| 0:40 | Loop-mapping table | "The loop is Study, Spec, Build, Validate, Release, Prove. Every stage is something you already did at lab scale. Study reuses your M3 competitor table and M7 pricing. Spec is your M4 folder or an equivalent. You are not restarting; you are extending." |
| 1:30 | The five-dimension table | "Five dimensions, zero to five, three means meets. Spec quality, build discipline, evidence honesty, privacy or claim discipline, launch-readiness. Pass is eighty percent or better with nothing below three — and the second half of that rule is where students fail." |
| 2:20 | Evidence record template | "The evidence record is the required attachment: commands with their counts, artifact links with the repo at a tag, and a limitations list with the failures still in it. Not 'tests pass'. The command, its counts, its date." |
| 3:00 | A commit history view | "One honesty clause applies one last time. Use agents for everything — the course teaches that. But the record states what the agent did and what you verified. A fabricated result is the only automatic fail." |
| 3:40 | Demo beat table | "The demo is five minutes in four beats. Thirty seconds on the problem, two minutes walking the loop with one artifact per stage, two minutes of the thing running, and thirty seconds on evidence and limits. End on the limits: that is where a technical audience decides to trust you." |
| 4:30 | `03-content/m08-launch-capstone/lab.md` acceptance checklist | "Post your plan before you build it. Peer review first, then Lab M8 — and demo day in the cohort is also where your testimonials come from." |

**Demo cue.** Open a real `git log --oneline` and point at a failing-test-first commit and a later fix. The viewer should see that TDD leaves a shape in history, not just a claim in a README.

**Action-step close.** Post your capstone plan — archetype, one-sentence scope, each loop stage mapped to a named artifact, and a first-pass self-score naming your weakest dimension — and review one peer's plan by naming the missing artifact.

**Recording notes.**
- Show the evidence template as editable text; students copy this block verbatim.
- If over time, cut the loop-mapping walkthrough to two rows and keep the demo beats.
- Do not show or invent a student submission, score, or testimonial at any point.
