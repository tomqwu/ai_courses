# Pricing — Expertise as a Product (Track Bundle)

> **Price: $199, self-paced, one-time, lifetime access.**
> This record follows the decision format of the course's own pricing file (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`): floor, comparator band, why not cheaper, why not more expensive, launch policy, and the honest-marketing checklist. Every benchmark cites `course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md`.

## The decision in one line

$199 makes this a **single-archetype entry product**, deliberately cheaper than the full course and deliberately not a substitute for it. **The full course at $399 is the recommended path and the better value** — it costs $200 more and adds two product archetypes, five labs, and 32 more quiz questions. Anyone who wants to build and sell more than one product type should buy the full course.

## Cost floor

The course's lean month-1 floor is **$80–130/month** — platform, community, and email, plus per-enrollment fees (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Cost floor"). At $399 that breaks even at ~2 sales/month. At $199 it breaks even at **under 1 sale/month**, which is the honest reason a single-track bundle can exist at this price at all. A price below the floor is not a price; it is a subsidy.

## Comparator band

The research's rule of thumb for self-paced content is **$97–297**, with live cohorts at **$500–2,000+** (`course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md`, §C, citing ShopSpace). $199 sits in the upper half of the self-paced band: above the $97–197 zone, where completion runs 18–25%, and below the $297–497 zone, where it runs ~38–42% (Ruzuku, §B). Marketplace data adds the direction: courses priced ≥$950 earn 50–100% more per landing-page visit — but that is a live-cohort argument, and this is not a live product.

Against comparable self-paced technical offerings, $199 is unremarkable and that is the point. It is not priced to signal luxury; it is priced to be an easy yes for someone testing one archetype.

## Why not cheaper

- **The research says a bare library should not be sold at all.** "A stack of Zoom recordings is not a self-paced course" (§C). This bundle ships with objective lab checklists, a peer-review requirement, a capstone, and lifetime access — the artifacts the rule says justify a self-paced price. Dropping below $199 would invite the comparison to a bare video library, which is the comparison the bundle loses.
- **Price drives completion.** $97–197 programs complete at 18–25%; $297–497 programs complete at ~38–42% (§B). Completion is the product here: a student who doesn't ship has bought nothing. A "bargain" price would reduce the outcome we are selling.
- **A $99 bundle would cannibalize the full course upward.** At $99, buyers who need only archetype 3 stop there; at $199 the gap to the full course is small enough that multi-archetype buyers sensibly take the full course — which is what we want them to do.

## Why not more expensive

- **The bundle is 1 of 3 archetypes.** Pricing a single track near the full course ($399) would collapse the ladder and make the full course look like a rounding error. The gap must be wide enough to be a real decision, and the full course must win it. At $199 the comparison is fair: $200 more buys two more archetypes and five more labs.
- **No testimonials yet.** The course holds $1,490 rather than $1,800+ for exactly this reason — "raising price before social proof exists inverts the trust order" (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Why not more expensive"). The same discipline applies one tier down.

## Anti-cannibalization rules (binding on the storefront)

1. **The full course is presented as the recommended path on the bundle's own sales page, above the fold and again in the pricing block** (`sales-page.md`, sections 1 and 8). The recommendation is not buried.
2. **No upgrade credit.** Buying the bundle and later the full course costs $598. A credit would make the two-step path exactly as cheap as buying the full course directly, which would reward indecision and invert the ladder. The honest framing is stated plainly: if you want more than one archetype, buy the full course now.
3. **No bundle discount stacking.** Launch policy below applies to one tier at a time.
4. **The bundle never advertises the material it excludes as "coming."** It says what it is: one archetype, six weeks, four labs (M0, M1, M6, M7).

## Launch policy

- Launch-week price for the bundle: **$149 (25% off), 72 hours, deadline stated plainly.** The research's pattern is that 42–55% of enrollments arrive in the final 48 hours (§E), which is precisely why the deadline must be real: a countdown that resets trains buyers to wait and makes the *next* launch's final 48 hours worthless.
- No flash sales that devalue the full course or the cohort. The Udemy $9.99 spiral — "it trains buyers to wait" — is the cautionary tale (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`).
- Refund policy: 14 days, keep the materials. Generous refunds are a conversion asset for trust-sensitive technical buyers, and the capstone-based value makes abuse rare.

## Revenue sanity check (plan, not a promise)

At a conservative 12% page conversion on warm traffic, the bundle needs ~17 sales to match the revenue of 8 full-course sales at $399. That asymmetry is the reason for the anti-cannibalization rules above: the bundle exists to *qualify* single-archetype buyers, not to compete with the full course for the same buyer. These are plans, not promises; record actuals after each launch and re-derive.

## Honest-marketing checklist (run on every asset)

1. Every number carries a source (repo file or cited research) — including every price in this record.
2. Qualify illustrative vs. observed. The ai_qe funnel stages cited in `sales-page.md` are planned and recorded stages, not delivered client results (`ai_qe/README.md`, `ai_qe/_data/engagement.json`).
3. Never invent testimonials. Reserved slots only, until real buyers exist.
4. State refund and deadline policies plainly; no fake countdowns.
5. Price the transformation and the artifacts — the lab checkpoints, the reviews, the capstone — not the video hours.
