# Pricing — Spec-Driven AI SaaS Track Bundle

> The decision record behind the $199 price. Every benchmark traces to `course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md` or the course's own pricing record, `course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`. This bundle does not invent a new pricing philosophy; it applies the existing one to a subset.

## The price

| SKU | Price | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Spec-Driven AI SaaS (this bundle)** | **$199** | M0, M1, M4, M5 + the M7/M8 launch slice · 4 labs · 4 quizzes (32 questions) · lifetime access · no cohort, no code review |
| **AI Product Studio (full course, self-paced)** | **$399** | All 9 modules · 3 product tracks · 8 labs · 72 quiz questions · capstone rubric and demo requirement |
| **Studio Live (8-week cohort)** | **$1,490** (founding $990) | Full course + weekly workshops + instructor code review on 3 labs + capstone review and demo day |
| **Team** | **$2,500–4,000** (3–5 seats) | Private cohort or workshop + private code review + team capstone review |

The bundle is **self-paced only**. No live component is available in this SKU; live access starts at the $1,490 cohort tier.

## Why $199

**It sits inside the self-paced band, above the median.** The research's rule of thumb is "$97–297 self-paced, or $500–2,000+ as a live 4-week cohort" (`course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md` §C, citing ShopSpace), and the platform-wide median one-time course price is $150 (IQR $49–357; §C). $199 is a professional price inside the band and above the median — high enough to signal a serious product, low enough to be an easy yes for a working engineer.

**It is priced off a transformation, not a recording.** The research's verdict on the alternative is blunt: a bare video library "shouldn't be sold at all" — "a stack of Zoom recordings is not a self-paced course" (§C). The bundle ships four pass/fail labs with objective checklists, a 6-week schedule, a stranger-test gate, and a capstone worksheet. The artifacts are the product.

**It is a subset price, and it must be below $399.** The full course's Studio tier is $399 (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "The price ladder"). A single-track subset cannot be priced at or near the full course, or the ladder inverts and the full course stops selling.

## Why the full course is the better value — stated plainly

This is the anti-cannibalization argument, and it is arithmetic rather than rhetoric:

- **Same per-module rate, more product.** The full course is $399 for 9 modules ≈ **$44.33 per module**. This bundle is $199 for roughly 4.5 modules (M0, M1, M4, M5, and the M7/M8 launch slice) ≈ **$44.22 per module**. The bundle buyer gets **no discount** for accepting a subset. There is no rational reason to buy the bundle *instead of* the full course if you want more than the SaaS track — which is exactly the property a non-cannibalizing subset price must have.
- **The bundle excludes the capstone.** The full course's assessment is labs 60% / quizzes 20% / **capstone 20%** (`course/01-design/content-standards.md` §2.7). M8.3 — the shipped product, the evidence record, the 5-minute demo, demo day — is not in this bundle. The missing fifth of the assessment is the part that produces the portfolio artifact.
- **Two of three product archetypes are absent.** M2–M3 (the on-device AI app: capture pipeline, fail-closed privacy mode, real-LLM contract tests, notarized releases) and M6 (the expertise product: provenance, audience routing, editioned releases) are full tracks the bundle does not contain.
- **No upgrade credit is offered in this release.** Do not buy this bundle expecting to apply $199 toward the $399 course later; no such policy exists here. If you want the other two tracks or the capstone, buy the full course up front.
- **The sales page says so.** Section 8 of `sales-page.md` recommends the $399 course in the same pricing table, and the README repeats it. The bundle is not a trap door into an upsell; it is the narrow door for buyers who specifically want the SaaS track and nothing else.

**Recommended-path rule.** Wherever the bundle is sold, the $399 full course is presented as the recommended path for buyers who want more than one product type or who want a capstone artifact. If a page can only show one option, it shows the full course.

## Why not cheaper

- **Completion economics.** Programs priced $97–197 complete at 18–25%; $297–497 complete at ~38–42%; $500–1,500+ at 53–68% (`course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md` §B). This course's product *is* completion — a student who does not run the labs has bought nothing. Honest caveat: **$199 sits just below the $297 tier where the completion rate roughly doubles.** That is a known weakness of this price point, and the bundle compensates with the three levers the research identifies: a fixed one-module-per-week schedule, objective lab checkpoints, and lesson-level discussion prompts (the +14-point completion lever, §B). If completion data after the first cohort disappoints, the first fix to evaluate is $249–$299 — not a discount.
- **Marketplace signal.** Courses priced ≥$950 on professional marketplaces earn 50–100% more per landing-page visit than cheaper ones (§C). Pricing at $49 or $97 to move units would cost the professional positioning the whole APS-3 family depends on.
- **A price below the cost floor is a subsidy.** The lean month-1 cost floor — course platform + community + email — is ≈$80–130/month (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Cost floor"). At $199, one sale a month covers fixed costs (derived: $80–130 ÷ $199 ≈ 0.4–0.7). At $19 it would take five to seven.

## Why not more expensive

- **No public testimonials yet.** The full course's own record says it: "raising price before social proof exists inverts the trust order" (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Why not more expensive (yet)"). A bundle with no students has no outcomes to anchor a higher price to.
- **It is a subset.** Charging above the full course's per-module rate would be indefensible on the page.

## Launch-price policy

- Follow the existing record: no flash-sale pricing, no recurring "last chance," and no countdown that resets. The full course's policy bans the Udemy $9.99 pattern because it "trains buyers to wait" (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Launch-price policy").
- If an introductory bundle price is run, it must be stated with a real end date and traded for something specific (a testimonial plus a feedback session is the trade the full course used for its founding cohort). **Open decision, not yet set:** no introductory price for this bundle is recorded as of this file. Recording one here is a prerequisite to advertising one.
- Refunds: full refund within 14 days, keep the materials (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Money-back guarantee"). This is a conversion asset, not a concession.

## Platform

Sell the bundle on the self-paced platform already chosen for this product family — Thinkific (or Teachable) — with the community layer on Circle or Discord. Do **not** make Udemy the primary channel: 37% marketplace payout, platform-controlled $9.99 pricing, and no student-email export (`course/00-research/02-course-market-research.md` §D; `course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Platform decisions").

## Honest-marketing checklist (gate for every asset)

1. Every number carries a source — a repo file or a cited research benchmark.
2. Qualify what is illustrative versus observed.
3. Never invent testimonials; the sales page reserves slots instead (`sales-page.md` §6).
4. State refund and deadline policies plainly; no fake countdowns.
5. Price the transformation and the artifacts — labs, checklists, the stranger-test gate — not video hours.

## What is not verified

- **No bundle sales data exists.** This price is a design decision derived from cited benchmarks, not an observed outcome. Record actuals after launch and re-derive the model — the course's own evidence discipline applied to its business (`course/04-sales/pricing-and-platforms.md`, "Revenue model").
- **No testimonials or student outcomes exist for this bundle.** None are claimed anywhere in these five files.
- **Break-even arithmetic is derived**, not observed: it divides the documented $80–130/month cost floor by the $199 price.
